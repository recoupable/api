# Digital experience reference library

Research date: 28 September 2026. **60 references across 10 categories.** Research candidates, not customer-approved taste, production templates or an empirical ranking of the best-performing experiences.

Open [the searchable library](index.html). It is self-contained and works locally, with filters for category, production shape and a 22-reference starting selection. Each card links to its source and records the participant's action, our interpretation of the appeal, a transferable pattern and the production dependency that is easy to overlook. Expand a card to see the latter two.

[Structured source data](library.json) · Rebuild the HTML with `python3 docs/sites/references/build-library.py` from the API repository root.

## What this collection is for

The immediate objective is to establish better reference points before choosing prompts or templates. It deliberately includes small games, tools, editorial work, installations and sustained campaigns. Music is one category, not the organizing principle. This research does not change the production Sites engine.

A useful reference gives us something concrete to discuss: the action, the quality of its response and the reason to do it again, continue or share. A visually impressive opening screen alone cannot answer those questions. Conversely, an experience can succeed as a short, satisfying encounter without maximizing time spent.

## Working findings — design interpretations to test

### 1. A familiar action can support an original experience

XOX retains recognizable rules; Click Click Click transforms mundane browser behavior through authored responses. The lesson is not to invent a new interaction every time. An understandable action leaves room for personality, content and execution. This also argues against blanket bans on quizzes, taps, cards or branching choices: inspect the actual choice and payoff.

### 2. The reward needs substance

Townscaper makes a place. Incredibox makes a performance. Mon Top Netflix makes an opinion visible. Their outputs are different, but all give participation a concrete consequence. A generic label or flattering sentence should not be treated as equivalent merely because it appears on a share card.

### 3. Good defaults can make people feel capable

Silk, AutoDraw and Blob Opera hide difficult craft behind accessible inputs. These are not simply blank canvases with more controls. The system helps the participant produce something coherent early, then leaves space for variation.

### 4. Curiosity requires something to discover

Space Elevator, the Magritte project and A Dark Room offer changing material as people progress. “Explore a world” is incomplete as a concept until the designer can say what the visitor discovers and why that discovery matters.

### 5. Social participation is a mechanism, not a share button

Gartic Phone changes because of other people. Your Line or Mine assembles individual marks into a larger work. Do Not Touch exposes earlier participation inside the experience. These require an actual contribution or coordination system; decorative avatars or invented crowd statistics would not deliver the same value.

### 6. Personalization is strongest when its inputs matter

Wrapped and Strava use real history. Netflix asks for an actual opinion. LEGO provides expressive parts. These are different forms of personalization and should be described honestly. A short questionnaire can produce an entertaining interpretation; it should not imply knowledge of behavior it never observed.

### 7. Production craft is often the missing ingredient

References with simple interfaces can depend on extensive animation, illustration, audio, physics, data preparation or editorial work. The library flags those dependencies so a later concept generator cannot borrow the promise while omitting the capability that fulfills it.

### 8. An ending can be a good outcome

A short story, a completed creation or a satisfying reveal may have done its job when the visitor leaves. Touch for Luck is explicitly a critique of prolonged, reward-driven attention. Retention should not become the only definition of quality.

## What not to conclude yet

- These references have not been approved as the customer's taste.
- Attractive case studies and awards do not establish causal engagement or conversion improvements.
- Historical campaigns are not assumed to remain playable. Follow the creator source for demonstrations when the original is unavailable.
- A broad pattern is transferable; proprietary art, audio, code and brand assets are not automatically reusable.
- The listed production shapes are qualitative assessments, not cost estimates or claims that Sites already supports them.
- The engine's current concept quality has not been measured against this collection.

## Evidence and coverage

Sources prioritize the creators, publishers, brands and product pages. The action field summarizes documented behavior; appeal, borrowing and dependency fields are our analysis. One round of Absurd Trolley Problems was actually tried, including choosing an answer and observing the comparison result. Other entries were researched, not exhaustively playtested. The NYC Textscape page exposed limited text; its search premise is documented in [The Pudding's publisher index](https://pudding.cool/).

The collection intentionally spans ten categories: quick challenges; games that unfold; creative toys; identity and personal output; social and collective experiences; brand games and campaigns; physical plus digital; exploration and stories; music and performance; and learning by doing. These categories describe the primary angle of study, not rigid genre boundaries.

## Next use of the collection

First collect reactions to actual references: **keep / reject / borrow this part**, with a concrete reason. Then compare a few directions using the same source context. Only after a direction demonstrates a worthwhile interaction should we decide which implementation pieces to turn into reusable foundations. No additional automated judge, rigid template catalog or production prompt rewrite is introduced by this research.
