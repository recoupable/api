Recoup / Field notes 001Reference collection · 28 Sep 2026
Play / Make / Discover / Belong

60 ways to
earn attention.

A broad collection of digital experiences worth studying. Games, creative toys, brand worlds, personal stories and collective experiments—organized around what people actually do.

Research candidates, not approved templates. “Worth studying” is our design interpretation, not a measured ranking of engagement. Product pages and historical case studies are labeled; a surviving source does not establish that an old experience still runs.

60References
10 categories

Make the first touch count.

Start with Perfect Circle, Patatap, Quick, Draw! and XOX. Compare rules, feedback and the desire to try again.

Give people something worth making.

Compare Silk, Townscaper, Incredibox and See Yourself in Sound. The reward is the creation itself.

Make participation matter.

Study Gartic Phone, Your Line or Mine and Puff Up Club. Another person changes the experience.

Reward curiosity.

Explore Radio Garden, MoMA Magritte, A Dark Room and The Pudding. Each action reveals a reason to continue.

Use the collection to develop taste.

These are starting points for comparison, not instructions to copy. Keep the interaction, its content and its execution separate when discussing what works.

  1. Try a reference or watch the creator’s demonstration. Judge the first action and what it produces.
  2. Mark what you would keep, reject or borrow—and name the specific part.
  3. Compare an obvious, well-executed interaction with an ambitious one. Complexity is neither a requirement nor a disqualification.
  4. Only then decide whether a reusable foundation would preserve the part you liked.

Research method: Creator, publisher, brand and product sources were reviewed. One round of Absurd Trolley Problems was tried; the other experiences were researched from their pages and documentation, not end-to-end playtested. No campaign performance numbers are used as independent proof of quality. Scope labels and adaptation notes are our assessments, not capability claims about the current Sites engine.
