# First-time fan journey

The engine designs an invitation, first action, meaningful response and growing freedom. These are responsibilities, not a four-screen template. A game can begin with a forgiving practice move; a creative tool with a visible change; a story with a consequential choice; an ambient world with optional interaction.

## Implementation

- `openingSequence.ts` owns the structured plan and shared principles.
- New creative directions require an opening plan. Existing saved directions remain readable; builders apply the principles to their existing activity.
- The incremental builder receives the principles each turn, including after compaction. Its original instruction preserves the opening plan.
- `reviewOpeningSequence.ts` receives only initial mobile and desktop screenshots. It cannot read the concept, contract, implementation or solved journey. Its concrete implementation failures are merged into the existing repair loop; an art review cannot override them.
- The browser runner preserves an immediate first-action screenshot separately from later participation checkpoints. The informed interaction review uses that evidence to judge feedback against the intended opening.
- Opening findings are saved under review verification. No new public endpoint or schema migration is needed.

This is an agent visual review plus scripted interaction evidence, not a human usability study or a blind autonomous playthrough. Keyboard input at mobile dimensions does not prove touch usability. The isolated renderer does not exercise the host's Spotify authentication. Avoid claiming those checks passed.

## Research translated into principles

- [Apple: Onboarding for games](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/onboarding-for-games/): teach through interaction and introduce mechanics in context.
- [George Fan: How I Got My Mom to Play Through Plants vs. Zombies (slides)](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2012/slides/Design%20Track/Fan_George_How%20I%20Got.pdf): staged teaching, forgiving early play and concise instruction.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/): expose the useful next choice without overwhelming the first view.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/): help close to the task rather than a disconnected tour.
- [Portal creators: Thinking with Portals](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/thinking-with-portals-creating-valve-s-new-ip): teach mechanics through designed situations and observable consequences.

Our synthesis: intrigue about the outcome is welcome; uncertainty about what to do is a defect. Do not demand a welcome modal, tutorial carousel, narrative prologue or signup wall for every format. Preserve the selected concept and fix the entrance with the smallest effective change.
