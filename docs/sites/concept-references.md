# Sites concept calibration — reference shortlist

Status: research candidates, not customer-approved examples. Reviewed 2026-09-28 from the linked creators' pages. Historical case studies are not evidence that their original interactive sites still work. No reference has been copied or implemented here.

## Working recommendation

Start with references before locking in templates. Separate the quality of a core interaction from its visual treatment and from its fit to a particular song. A template can stabilize controls, layout and rendering; it cannot supply a good joke, artist connection or reason to participate.

Ask the customer to mark each reference keep / reject / keep one part, with one concrete reason. Store those decisions separately from the engine's self-assessment. Until then, do not treat these examples or a preference for playful work as approved taste.

## Candidate references

| Reference | What the creator documents | Proposed lesson (our interpretation) | What must not be assumed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Patatap](https://patatap.com/) | Touch creates sound and moving shapes. | Immediate, rich feedback can make a simple action worthwhile. | Random tapping alone is not the mechanic's appeal. A song-specific version needs suitable playable sounds and visual assets. |
| [Blob Opera](https://artsandculture.google.com/experiment/blob-opera/AAHWrq360NcGbw) | Dragging changes pitch and vowels; other voices harmonize using ML. | Direct manipulation and responsive characters let a novice get an expressive result immediately. | A static illustration plus sliders cannot reproduce the musical behavior. Its ML model is not an available Sites capability. |
| [Draw a Perfect Circle](https://neal.fun/perfect-circle/) | One drawing challenge and a percentage result. | An instantly understandable rule and visible skill gap create a reason to try again. | Adding an artist logo to an unrelated challenge does not create a song connection. |
| [Song Maker](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/song-maker) | A tool for making and sharing songs. | The user's actions create an actual artifact they can hear. | A song-themed editor is not automatically desirable; there must be a reason to make that particular thing and a supported playback path. |
| [Porter Robinson: Look at the Sky — creator case study](https://activetheory.medium.com/look-at-the-sky-b03ac8d48252) | A shared listening environment evolves with the song; listeners look around and draw trails. | The song and artist world can provide the experience itself without a quiz or score. | Multiplayer, WebXR, synchronized playback and lyric timing require capabilities beyond the current isolated Sites experience. Use as an aspirational art-direction reference, not a ready template. |

## Calibration method

1. Compare the first action and its actual result, not just the opening screenshot.
2. Record separately: desire to participate, clarity, response quality, song/artist fit, and visual execution. Do not collapse these into a made-up overall score.
3. Pair a chosen reference with a weak generated example and explain the specific difference. For the current invitation comic, assess the actual writing and scene changes; do not declare every branching story bad.
4. Once references are approved, prototype two or three different mechanics against the same saved song context. Keep the input constant so differences are interpretable.
5. Promote only a successful prototype into a reusable implementation foundation. The engine then supplies release-specific content, artwork and variations within supported capabilities.
